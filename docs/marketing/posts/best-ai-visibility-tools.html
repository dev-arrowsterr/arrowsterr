What is an AI visibility tool?

An AI visibility tool tracks how often AI assistants mention your brand when people ask about your category. You’ll also hear them called AI rank trackers, LLM tracking tools or GEO tools. The names differ, and the core job is roughly the same.

You give the tool a list of prompts, such as “best CRM for real estate agents.” It asks those prompts in ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity and Google’s AI Overviews, then records:

  • whether your brand was named,
  • where it showed up in the list,
  • how warmly the AI described it,
  • which competitors were named instead,
  • which websites the AI cited as sources.

Think of it as rank tracking for answers. Google rankings tell you where your page sits on a results page. AI visibility tells you whether you’re part of the answer at all.

Why is AI visibility so hard to measure?

If you’ve tried to track this by hand, you already know it gets messy fast. Four things make it hard.

AI answers don’t stay still

Ask ChatGPT the same question twice and you may get two different lists. The models are designed to vary their answers a little each time, and they get updated often. A single check tells you very little, so you need many checks over time to see the real pattern.

There’s no Search Console for ChatGPT

Google gives you Search Console, which shows the queries your site appeared for and how often people clicked. No AI assistant shares that kind of data. You have to work out which questions your buyers ask, then test them yourself.

Small wording changes flip the result

“Best CRM for agencies” and “which CRM should a small agency use” mean the same thing to you. A language model can answer them with different brands. That’s why good tools track groups of related prompts, called topics, and report on each group as a whole.

The answer depends on the sources

When an AI assistant searches the web before answering, it pulls from a handful of pages such as review sites, Reddit threads, listicles and brand websites. If those pages don’t mention you, the answer won’t either. A good tool shows you those sources so you know where you need to appear.

How AI visibility tools work

Under the hood, almost every tool follows the same four steps.

  1. Ask the prompts. The tool sends your prompts to each AI, through its API or through a browser that acts like a real user. The better tools run them every day, since a weekly check misses too much movement.
  2. Read every answer. It pulls out every brand each answer names, in order, and matches spelling variations so “HubSpot CRM” and “HubSpot” count as one brand.
  3. Score the results. Most tools roll the answers into visibility (the share of answers that name you), position, sentiment and citations.
  4. Track the trend. Because answers move around, the trend over 7, 30 and 90 days matters far more than any single day.

Not sure which prompts to track? Here’s a trick I use with every client. Export your queries from Google Search Console, paste them into a Google Sheet and filter for questions with this regex:

\b(who|what|where|when|why|how|which|can|do|does|is|are|should|best|top|vs)\b

Long questions that already bring you Google traffic are often the same ones people now ask AI. You’ll end up with prompts like “Which CRM do most realtors use?” or “Is HubSpot or Pipedrive better for a small sales team?”

One check is a coin flip. Thirty days of daily checks is data you can act on.

What to look for in an AI visibility tool

I’ve tested a lot of these tools, and the gap between the good ones and the rest is wide. Here’s the checklist I run through before I recommend one to a client.

  1. You can try it first. A free trial says a lot about how confident a team is in its product.
  2. It suggests prompts. Knowing what to track is the hardest part, and tools that research your brand and suggest prompts save you hours.
  3. It checks the models your buyers use. ChatGPT is a must. Google AI Overviews and AI Mode matter for anyone with Google traffic, and Perplexity, Gemini and Claude round out the list. Check whether any of them cost extra.
  4. It shows sources. Visibility numbers tell you where you stand. Sources tell you what to do about it.
  5. It helps you act. The best tools turn gaps into a content plan or a list of sites to get mentioned on. A chart on its own doesn’t grow anything.

How to get your brand recommended by AI

Tracking is step one. Here’s what I do once the data comes in. For tools focused on this side of the work, see our guide to the best GEO tools.

Understand query fan-out

When you ask an AI assistant a question, it often splits it into several smaller searches before it answers. This is called query fan-out. Ask “what’s the best CRM for a small agency?” and the model might also search for “CRM pricing for small teams,” “CRM with client portals” and “HubSpot alternatives.”

It then blends the best parts of each search into one answer. That’s why pages with almost no Google traffic can still get cited. Ahrefs’ Ryan Law looked at the 1,000 pages ChatGPT cited most and found that 28.3% of them had zero organic traffic. If your page answers one of those smaller questions really well, you can win the citation.

Go where AI is already looking

Open the sources report in your tool and list the sites AI cites most for your topics. In most B2B categories you’ll see the same mix: G2 and Capterra, a few Reddit threads, two or three “best tools” listicles and some brand sites.

Each of those is a target. Ask happy customers for reviews, join the Reddit conversations honestly and pitch the authors of those listicles. One new mention on a source AI trusts can move your visibility more than a month of new blog posts.

Write the pages buyers ask about

Look at the prompts where competitors get named and you don’t. They usually point to a handful of missing pages:

  • “Best [category] for [audience]” pages
  • “[Your brand] vs [competitor]” comparisons
  • “[Competitor] alternatives” pages
  • Clear pricing and feature pages that AI can quote

Write them with plain facts AI can lift: who it’s for, what it costs, what it does better and real numbers. Vague marketing copy rarely gets quoted.

Refresh pages that almost rank

Pages sitting at positions 4 to 20 on Google are your fastest wins. AI assistants often search the web behind the scenes, so climbing a few spots there can also lift you in AI answers. Update those pages with fresh data, clearer headings and direct answers to the questions in your prompts.

Measure, then repeat

Give every change two to four weeks, then check the visibility trend for that topic. Keep what works and do more of it.

AI visibility tools FAQ

Is AI visibility the same as SEO?

They overlap a lot, and strong SEO pages often get cited by AI too. AI visibility also depends on what other sites say about you and on how clearly your pages answer specific questions.

How many prompts should I track?

Start with 25 to 50 prompts spread across 5 or so topics. That’s enough to see patterns without drowning in data. Add more once you know which topics matter most.

How often should I check?

Daily checks are best, because answers shift so often. Then judge your progress by the 30-day trend.

Which AI assistants matter most?

ChatGPT has the most users, so start there. If you get traffic from Google, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode matter just as much. Then add Perplexity, Gemini and Claude.

What is the cheapest AI visibility tool?

GA4 is free, and AthenaHQ has a free tier with 300 credits. The cheapest paid plan is Keyword.com’s credit-based AI Visibility plan at $9.80 a month.
